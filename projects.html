"De-Haloing" Presidential Candidates Using CFA (Nov-Dec 2024)

In our final project for Marketing Analytics, we analyzed 2008 presidential election data to investigate the halo effect's influence on people's overall impressions of presidential candidates. To explore this phenomenon, we employed confirmatory factor analysis (CFA). Initially, we constructed a two-factor model to represent perceptions of domestic policy and foreign policy. Using this model, we performed a regression analysis to examine the impact of these two factors on overall impression. To account for the halo effect, we introduced a third factor, effect, into the model. When we reran the regression analysis on this three-factor model, we observed that the p-values of domestic and foreign policy became completely insignificant, suggesting that the halo effect was driving overall impressions rather than opinions on specific policy areas. Working with binary survey data added a layer of complexity to the analysis, but this challenge deepened my understanding of factor analysis techniques. One of my key takeaways from this project was developing a clear grasp of both principal components analysis (PCA) and confirmatory factor analysis (CFA), and learning how to apply these techniques to derive actionable insights. Pricing Strategies Using Non-Linear Programming (Nov 2024)

The goal of this project was to investigate the newsvendor model (NVM) in the context of manufacturing operations, focusing on meeting demand while avoiding overproduction. We began by performing a linear regression to model the relationship between price and demand. Using the resulting coefficients, we generated demand data to account for variability. Next, we reformulated the classical NVM into a linear program (LP) to determine the optimal production quantity. Finally, we extended the model to a quadratic program (QP) to jointly optimize price and production quantity. Our analysis revealed that the choice of model depends on business priorities. The standard NVM is straightforward and efficient, making it ideal for scenarios where simplicity is critical. In contrast, the extended model's dynamic pricing strategies can enhance profitability but come with added complexity. My key takeaway from this project was understanding the tradeoffs between simplicity and profitability. While simplicity is often more effective in resource-constrained environments, the NVM highlights the importance of balancing this simplicity with strategies to minimize oversupply and maximize operational efficiency.

Creating a Crowdsourced Beer Recommender (Oct 2024)

The goal of this assignment was to develop a crowdsourced recommender system that provides users with three personalized beer recommendations. We began by scraping reviews from BeerAdvocate.com to build the recommendation engine. To generate recommendations, we conducted a similarity analysis between user-specified attributes and the review data, combined with sentiment analysis to create an evaluation metric. We also compared the results of using word embeddings versus a bag-of-words model to assess the impact of these approaches on recommendation quality. This project explored the long tail effect, focusing not just on recommending popular beers but on identifying options that closely align with individual customer preferences, showcasing the potential of tailored recommendations.

Competitive Analysis of Entry-Level Luxury Cars (Oct 2024)

The goal of this assignment was to conduct a competitive analysis of the entry-level luxury car market in the United States. We analyzed online discussions from Edmunds.com forums, using Python to scrape approximately 5,000 posts. After removing stop words, we identified the most frequently mentioned words and calculated lift ratios among the top 10 car brands to determine which brands were often discussed together. To enhance visualization, we created a Multi-Dimensional Scaling (MDS) plot to map brand relationships. Based on our analysis, we provided actionable insights and recommendations for the market.

Financial Portfolio Optimization (Oct 2024)

The goal of this project was to construct an optimized stock portfolio that effectively tracks the NASDAQ-100. We aimed to determine the optimal number of stocks to include in the portfolio and their respective weights to minimize tracking error relative to the index. We approached the problem using two methods: a combination of integer programming and linear programming, and mixed integer programming. To evaluate the performance of each method, we analyzed how tracking error varied as the number of stocks in the portfolio changed. After comparing the results, we recommended implementing the mixed integer programming method, as it provided clearer guidance on the optimal number of stocks and consistently resulted in lower tracking errors. One of the key challenges in this project was addressing missing data in the stock market context, and I learned how data imputation influences the problem-solving process. Our team chose to handle missing data using forward and backward filling, which ensured a continuous time series that supported accurate analysis. This project enhanced my understanding of optimization techniques and the critical role of data preprocessing in financial modeling.

Image Segmentation using LP (Sept 2024)

The goal of this project was to separate an image into foreground and background using linear programming. We transformed the image into a network and applied the max flow-min cut theorem to determine the optimal separation between the foreground and background. The problem was solved by formulating the linear programming model in Gurobi using Python. The most challenging aspect of this project was adapting the Gurobi optimization to work across different types of images. We began with simple geometric shapes and progressively tackled more complex images. My key takeaway from this project was learning to think creatively and apply linear programming techniques to new challenges, such as image processing. This experience deepened my understanding of how optimization can be used beyond traditional applications.

Predicting Rent Prices (Apr 2023 - May 2023)

The goal of the project was to recommend the top 5 ideal zip codes for a recent college graduate to live in, based on the difference between the median income level in the city and the predicted rent price. In this project, I transformed a data frame with 674 observations and 19 variables by identifying outliers and deciding whether to keep or remove them, as well as normalizing numerical values. To predict rent prices, I trained five different predictive models, including GLMnet, a neural network, random forest, and both radial and linear support vector machines. I evaluated each model’s performance based on the mean absolute error, mean squared error, and R-squared.
